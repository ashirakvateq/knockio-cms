01

The company

Modern Roofing was founded in Pelzer, South Carolina in February 2014 and has grown from a home office into a 15-location operation spanning five states, with more than 10,600 roofs completed. It holds GAF Master Elite certification — a credential fewer than 2% of US contractors carry — and offers the Golden Pledge Warranty, GAF's highest residential warranty.

Two things about how the company operates matter more than its size.

It runs on insurance claims. Modern Roofing's stated promise to homeowners is a new roof without the insurance headaches: the company documents storm damage, submits it to the carrier, and attends the adjuster visit. Photographic evidence isn't a nice-to-have in that model. It is the product.

It runs its own crews. Where much of the industry subcontracts installation, Modern Roofing employs its crews directly and treats paying them fairly and on time as a core commitment. That makes labour a real, tracked cost rather than a line item passed through to a sub.

Steven Purdy manages a district within this structure — which places him precisely where canvassing, insurance documentation, production and collections are supposed to hand off to one another.

02

The before state: five systems, one homeowner

Modern Roofing had not chosen badly. It had chosen five reasonable tools, one at a time, each solving the problem in front of it at that moment. The result was five tools that each worked and did not work together.

Tool What it held What it couldn't see
Google Voice Calls and texts, under individual employee accounts The customer record, the job, or anyone else's conversation
GoHighLevel Marketing campaigns and inbound leads What happened after the lead was exported
Canvassing app Territories, door pins, inspection photos The estimate, the claim, the install, the invoice
Job management platform Scheduling, work orders, invoicing Where the lead came from or what was photographed at the door
Time tracking tool Crew hours Which job those hours belonged to

Between each of these sat a person, a spreadsheet, or a Zapier chain. The homeowner moved through all five. Their data did not.

03

Where it actually hurt: the claim chain

Modern Roofing's published process runs Day 1 inspection → Days 2–5 estimate → Weeks 2–6 claim approval → install day. Following one storm-damage job through the old stack shows where the money went.

  1. Day 1

    The inspection

    A rep knocks a door in an assigned territory, performs the free inspection, and photographs hail impacts, granule loss, missing shingles and compromised flashing. Those photos land in the canvassing app.

  2. Days 2–5

    The estimate

    The estimate and carrier documentation are built in the job management platform. The photos are in the canvassing app. Someone downloads, renames and re-uploads them. Customer name, address, phone and scope are retyped by hand. Every job, every time.

  3. The adjuster visit

    Assembling the packet

    Modern Roofing attends the adjuster meeting — this is a stated differentiator. Doing it well requires having every documented image, measurement and note in one place at one time. Assembling that packet was manual work performed under time pressure, and anything that had not made the jump between systems simply wasn't in the room.

  4. Weeks 2–6

    The waiting

    The longest and most fragile stage. Follow-up during this window happened over Google Voice, in individual employees' accounts. There was no shared record of what had been said, no automated cadence, and nothing to prompt a check-in. Jobs went quiet and nobody knew until they were gone.

  5. Install day

    Working from a copy

    The crew worked from the version of the scope that lived in the job platform, which was a retyped copy of what the rep had agreed at the door. Discrepancies surfaced on the roof.

  6. Invoicing

    Two payers, three systems

    The carrier and the homeowner's deductible, sometimes financed at 0%, reconciled by hand against a job whose full history lived in three systems.

  7. Payroll

    Job costing after the fact

    Crew hours sat in a fourth system with no link to the job, so the true labour cost of a roof was a monthly reconciliation exercise rather than a fact.

And then a storm hits

The site carries a live weather alert banner and promises 24/7 emergency response with same-day tarping. Modern Roofing's own marketing calls it the 72-hour window that matters.

A storm produces a surge in GoHighLevel, which has to be moved into the canvassing app, allocated across territories, and dispatched — while the same office staff are still hand-carrying yesterday's jobs between systems. The moment the company most needed to move fast was the moment its stack was slowest.

05

Evaluation

Purdy's requirement list was short.

  1. Canvassing and job management in the same database — not integrated, the same
  2. Photos captured at the door usable in the estimate immediately, with annotation for adjuster documentation
  3. Calls, texts and email attached to the customer record rather than to employees
  4. Territory and GPS visibility that stayed connected to downstream job outcomes
  5. Estimates, e-signature, work orders, invoicing and payment in one chain
  6. Role-based permissions across canvassers, project managers, field supervisors, office support and finance
  7. Mobile capability strong enough that reps would use it standing on a roof
  8. Existing GoHighLevel campaigns preserved, not rebuilt
  9. No long-term contract

Knockio met all nine in one system. Where ServiceTitan, Jobber and Workiz are built primarily around service operations, and SalesRabbit and Spotio primarily around canvassing, Knockio holds both sides of the same job — which was exactly the seam Modern Roofing was losing money in.

Two capabilities decided it. Media management with photo annotation meant inspection images could be marked up on site — arrows on lifted shingles, notes on granule loss — and stay attached to the lead, estimate, work order and claim documentation without being moved anywhere. And native GoHighLevel integration meant the marketing engine kept running while leads flowed directly into the pipeline instead of being exported.

Monthly billing, automatic volume pricing above five users, no setup fee and no long-term commitment meant the switch didn't require betting the district on it.

06

The migration

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Mapping

    Field-by-field mapping from the canvassing app and job platform into Knockio's pipeline boards. Boards were built to mirror Modern Roofing's actual stages — Inspection, Estimate, Claim Submitted, Adjuster Scheduled, Approved, Production, Install, Invoiced, Collected — with each stage assigned to the department that owns it. X legacy fields were identified as unused and retired rather than migrated.

  2. Weeks 2–3

    Data

    Active customers, open jobs and in-flight claims migrated in full. Jobs closed more than X months prior were archived to export rather than imported, which cut migration volume substantially and eliminated years of accumulated duplicates.

  3. Week 3

    Communication cutover

    The existing business line was ported into Knockio. This is the step that ends Google Voice — once the number lives on the platform, call and text history attaches to the customer automatically with no employee action required.

  4. Weeks 3–4

    Pilot

    PILOT LOCATION ran Knockio in parallel with the legacy stack. The team built stage automations and permission roles against live storm jobs rather than a demo.

  5. Week 5

    Training by role

    Sales reps were trained on mobile only and never shown the desktop interface. Office support, project managers, field supervisors and finance each received their own session covering only their own boards.

  6. Week 6

    Cutover

    GoHighLevel connected via native integration. The four remaining tools shut off.

6 weeks from kickoff to legacy shutdown.

07

What was difficult

Two things. Leaving them out would make everything above less believable.

Reps resisted in week one. Reps with years of habit in the old canvassing app lost selling time relearning an interface. The fix was pairing pilot reps with the rest of the team rather than adding formal training. Confirm actual approach

Custom reports had to be rebuilt. Several reports leadership depended on didn't port. They were reconstructed within X weeks and in most cases came back better, because the underlying data now spanned door knock through collected payment for the first time. But there was a reporting gap in between, and it was uncomfortable.

10

Lessons from Steven Purdy

  1. Count the labour, not the licences

    The five subscriptions were $10,450 a month. The people working around the gaps between them cost nearly three times that. Any consolidation case built only on subscription line items understates the return badly.

  2. In insurance roofing, the photos decide everything

    Evidence captured at the door and evidence used to build the claim have to live in the same place. Every hour spent moving images between systems is an hour of exposure on the claim.

  3. Keep what works

    GoHighLevel was performing and was integrated, not replaced. Consolidation removes seams, not tools.

  4. Port the number early

    Communication cutover produced the most immediate benefit and the least resistance — customers noticed nothing, staff noticed everything.

  5. Train by role, not by product

    Reps saw mobile only. Finance saw finance only. Showing everyone the whole platform produces confusion, not competence.

  6. Archive aggressively

    Leaving pre-DATE closed jobs behind removed years of duplicates and shortened the migration. Old data can live in an export.
